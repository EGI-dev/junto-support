Junto Android privacy information

This Android supplement describes the Android app from build 28. Read it together with the Junto privacy policy, which identifies the controller and explains account data, your rights, retention and international processing.

Account, photos and social activity

Junto uses its Appwrite backend in Frankfurt for authentication, profiles, photos, captions, selected places and social activity such as friendships, tags, joins, likes, blocks and reports. Your email and account identifiers support sign-in and account management. Posts and selected places are visible to the app’s authorised social audience according to friendship and privacy settings; tagged and joining friends can connect an activity to their friends. Do not include information you do not want that audience to see.

Before a username or photo submission is sent to OpenAI for safety checking, the app explains that processing and asks you to continue. The moderation service receives the submitted content needed for the check. Cancelling preserves your form or draft.

Location and nearby places on Android

Location permission is used in the foreground to help find and attach a place. Android place searches send search coordinates and query details over HTTPS to public OpenStreetMap Overpass servers, including overpass-api.de, overpass.private.coffee and, for some reverse lookups, overpass.kumi.systems. These operators receive your IP address and may keep service logs under their own policies. A selected place and its coordinates are sent to Junto when you post. There is no background-location feature. You can deny location permission and use the manual place option.

Contacts and notifications

Contact matching is optional and asks for permission. Phone numbers from contacts you allow are normalised and sent over TLS to Junto for matching; address-book names stay on your phone. The backend records matching request counts rather than storing the uploaded contact list. Firebase Cloud Messaging and Appwrite use device or installation identifiers and a push token to route notifications. You can manage notification permission in Android settings.

Optional Android analytics

Mixpanel usage analytics is off by default. In Junto’s Settings → Privacy → Optional usage analytics, you can choose to enable it. With consent, usage events can include account and post identifiers, venue names, social interactions, and device/app metadata. The Android app disables Mixpanel IP-based geolocation, although the service necessarily receives network connection information. Turning analytics off stops future app analytics and clears unsent local events; it does not delete events already sent. Contact support to request deletion of previously sent analytics.

Permissions and security

The app asks for camera access to take photos, optional read-only contact access, foreground location and notifications as needed. It does not request microphone access for still photos or broad photo-library/storage access. Network requests use HTTPS/TLS. This protects data in transit; it is not end-to-end encryption between users.

Deletion, retention and contact

You can delete your account in Settings → Delete Account or request deletion without the app. Post expiry ends visibility and is not an immediate-erasure guarantee. Provider logs, backups and legally required records can have separate retention periods, as described in the main policy.
